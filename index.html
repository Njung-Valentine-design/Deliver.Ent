What is DELIVER?

Not a training course.
A decision engine.

DELIVER is not a quiz, a tutorial, or a certification prep tool. It is the first master enterprise execution simulator that puts you inside the real psychological and political conditions of project delivery and forces you to make decisions under pressure, with incomplete information, against a live clock.

Every turn presents a genuine enterprise scenario drawn from one of 16 industries. A stakeholder escalates. A vendor collapses. A regulatory hold lands. A budget freeze arrives without warning. You have four options, a crisis timer running, and nine metrics that will shift — often in directions you did not expect — depending on how you respond.

The game was built around a single design principle: delivery failure is never caused by one bad decision. It is caused by a pattern of decisions — passive when action was needed, aggressive when trust was the asset, focused on governance when speed was the weapon. DELIVER surfaces that pattern and holds you accountable to it.

"DELIVER doesn't teach PM frameworks. It trains judgment,critical thinking, emotional intelligence and proper communication."

How it works

One turn. Nine consequences.

Each turn represents a governance period — a week, a sprint, or a steering cycle depending on your career level and industry. The situation tab delivers your scenario: a named project, a specific tension reported by a named stakeholder, political intelligence from your team, and a live inbox full of pressure, risk, and noise.

You choose from four decision options — each labelled by type: Balanced, Aggressive, Passive, or Governance. Every decision affects all nine dashboard metrics simultaneously. Budget and schedule respond differently than morale and stakeholder trust. Some consequences are immediate. Others compound quietly over three or four turns until they become a crisis.

At senior levels, a fifth option unlocks — political navigation. Risk previews disappear. Consequences hit harder. The same decision that earned you XP at Level 3 can end your career at Level 8.

01
Read the Situation
A named scenario lands in the Situation tab. Your lead stakeholder has escalated something specific — not a generic risk, but a real-world operational challenge drawn from the industry you are working in. Industry-specific jargon, named roles, and real consequences.
02
Check Your Inbox
Eight categories of messages arrive each turn: pressure from above, risk flags from the team, positive signals, and administrative noise. Reading the inbox is information work — not everything matters, and separating signal from noise is part of the skill.
03
Understand Your People
Five stakeholders with individual trust levels, hidden agendas, memory of your last three decisions, and a live AI-style reaction after every move. High-trust stakeholders react differently than low-trust ones. At Level 7+ high-power stakeholders use senior-level language — "At this level, the board is watching."
04
Make Your Move
Choose A, B, C, D — or write a custom executive response in plain English. Each option is typed and scored by the rules engine. You can also see the decision type badge (Balanced / Aggressive / Passive / Governance) on each option so you understand the strategic register before committing.
05
Watch the Consequences
Nine metrics shift simultaneously. Delta chips float up from affected bars. If the consequence triggers a chain reaction — burnout spreading to quality, stakeholder decline spreading to reputation — you will see it named in the events log. Your coaching tab analyses the decision pattern and gives you a competency radar.
06
Survive the Crisis
At any turn from Level 3 onwards, a crisis can fire. A 90-second countdown starts. The crisis event is prefixed with your industry-specific context — in Construction it is a scaffold collapse, in Banking it is a regulatory breach, in Healthcare it is a patient safety incident. Every second without action degrades morale and stakeholder trust automatically.
🚨 Example Crisis — Banking
🔴 ALERT: A ransomware attack has encrypted a project server. IT is working the problem now.
A regulatory breach has been identified in the test environment. Legal is on the line. You have 90 seconds to decide.
A Balanced Activate the cyber incident response plan — brief the sponsor and isolate all affected systems immediately.
B Passive Give IT 24 hours to resolve before escalating — avoid panic in the organisation.
C Aggressive Demand IT restores operations within 4 hours or you escalate to the board directly.
D Governance Trigger the formal business continuity plan and notify the data protection officer as required.

Chain Reaction — how one decision cascades

Aggressive push → Burnout rises → Quality drops → Client complaint → Stakeholder trust falls → Sponsor withdrawing → Career ends

Industries

16 simulated sectors.
Each one different.

Every industry in DELIVER. is a distinct simulation ecosystem. Not just different names — different mechanics, different stakeholder cultures, different compliance systems, different crisis patterns, and different visual environments. The way a Banking project fails is nothing like the way a Construction project fails.

Each industry has its own primary mechanic — a live gauge that tracks the most critical dimension of that sector. In Healthcare it is Patient Safety Index (never allowed to drop below 50 without triggering a clinical review freeze). In Banking it is Compliance Score (aggressive decisions trigger automatic audit events). In Construction it is HSE Rating (a single safety incident triggers a mandatory site stand-down). These mechanics make each industry feel like a genuinely different operating environment.

Game Systems

Everything is connected.
Nothing is isolated.

DELIVER. is built around 17 interconnected engine objects. The rules engine scores every decision across all nine metrics, adjusts competency dimensions, shifts stakeholder trust, advances the career arc, checks achievements, and generates the next scenario — all before the next turn renders. Nothing is scripted. Everything is calculated.

The 9 Live Dashboard Metrics

💚 Delivery
Core project health. The primary win condition. If this and Stakeholder both fall below 35 for two consecutive turns, your career ends.
🤝 Stakeholder
Executive and sponsor confidence. Drops with passive avoidance and public failures. Recovers slowly — trust takes turns to rebuild.
💰 Budget
Spend against allocation. Aggressive decisions burn it fast. Zero means immediate game over. Shown in real dollar terms at senior levels.
🗓️ Schedule
Timeline health. Passive decisions compress the schedule quietly. Governance decisions slow velocity. Aggressive decisions accelerate at a cost.
😤 Burnout
Team fatigue. The only metric where high is bad. Above 70 impairs quality and delivery. Above 85 and the team starts making visible errors.
😊 Morale
Team energy and engagement. Low morale reduces delivery velocity, increases resistance to change, and makes crises harder to resolve.
⭐ Reputation
Your personal brand in the market. Above 70 unlocks headhunter offers and salary negotiations. Below 30 and executives stop backing your decisions publicly.
✅ Quality
Output and solution quality. Drops when burnout is high or passive decisions accumulate. Low quality reaches clients and triggers formal complaints.
📋 Compliance
Regulatory and governance health. Aggressive decisions in regulated industries erode it fast. Below 30 triggers audit events and PMO monitoring.

Career Entry Positions

Not everyone starts at
the bottom.

Before the game begins you choose your starting position — the level of seniority you are entering at. This is not cosmetic. It determines your starting salary, your available budget, the number of tabs and tools you begin with, the political pressure from turn one, and the complexity of the scenarios you face.

A Project Coordinator starts with a clean dashboard, six tabs, and forgiving turn limits. A PMO Director starts with metrics already under pressure, all sixteen tabs active, no risk previews on decisions, and a board that is watching from the first move. The same industry, the same framework — completely different games.

Credits & Technical

Built in a browser.
Runs everywhere.

DELIVER is a single HTML file — no server, no database, no install, no framework. Open it in any modern browser and it runs. The entire simulation engine, all content, all visuals, and all audio are contained in one self-sufficient file of approximately 400KB.

The game engine is built in vanilla JavaScript — a deterministic rules engine that calculates all consequences from explicit logic. No AI API calls. No external data. Everything you see is computed locally in your browser the moment you make a decision.

The 3D background scene uses Three.js r128 — 40 wireframe geometric objects that react to gameplay in real time. Crisis events turn the scene red and accelerate it. Level promotions expand it. The particle network, grid overlay, and scanline texture run as always-on CSS layers behind all screens.

Audio is generated procedurally using the Web Audio API — a kick drum, snare, hi-hats, bass line, and ambient pad chords all synthesised in the browser at three dynamic BPM moods: normal (72 BPM), tense (88 BPM when metrics are low), and crisis (100 BPM during active crisis events). Five WAV sound effects are base64-encoded inline.

Player data is saved to localStorage — supporting multiple user accounts with password hashing, career saves, leaderboard scores, and achievement records — all without a backend.

Why you should play the simulation

Most professionals spend their entire careers trapped inside a single operational ecosystem.

A real estate manager rarely experiences the regulatory pressure of banking. A retail leader never navigates healthcare compliance risk. A government project manager may never face the operational volatility of aviation or manufacturing.

Yet organizations continue expecting leaders to transition across industries as if judgment transfers automatically.

It does not.

Judgment is built through exposure, pressure, consequence, recovery, and repetition. Traditionally, that process takes years of expensive mistakes, failed projects, damaged stakeholder trust, and operational losses.

Njung V. Zuh recognized a structural flaw in professional development: modern education teaches frameworks, certifications teach terminology, but almost nothing trains leaders to think under pressure across industries.

DELIVER was created to close that gap.

Mission

To democratize enterprise leadership capability through a transparent, cross-industry simulation platform where professionals can develop strategic judgment, operational resilience, and decision-making maturity without risking real budgets, careers, or stakeholder confidence.

Vision

To build the world’s first enterprise leadership master simulator, a system where any professional, regardless of industry, title, or financial background, can simulate an entire leadership career across all sectors eg hospitality, banking, healthcare, aviation, government, manufacturing, logistics, and energy.

The objective is not entertainment. The objective is transferable executive judgment, improve critical thinking and build emotional intelligence .

Five-Year Direction

  • DELIVER becomes a recognized supplement and practical tool to certifications such as PMP®, PRINCE2®, and Agile programs.
  • Universities integrate the platform into MBA and project management curricula.
  • Corporate L&D divisions adopt DELIVER for leadership onboarding and capability assessment.
  • Organizations contribute custom industry scenarios through an open benchmark ecosystem.
  • AI integration supports adaptive coaching and analysis without replacing critical thinking or transparent rule systems.

The Philosophy Behind DELIVER

“You do not become an enterprise leader by memorizing frameworks. You become one by making the wrong decision under pressure, recovering from failure, and learning how consequence moves through systems before real stakeholders are affected.”

DELIVER exists between three incomplete learning models:

  • Case studies are too passive.
  • Games are too abstract.
  • Real work is too expensive to fail inside.

DELIVER was designed to fit the missing layer between educational knowledge and real world responsibility.
